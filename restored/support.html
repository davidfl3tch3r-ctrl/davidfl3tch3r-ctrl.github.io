How the score works

Every morning Restored compares last night with your own recent history: overnight heart rate variability (40% of the score), resting heart rate (20%), sleep duration and efficiency (25%), respiratory rate (10%) and how hard you went yesterday (5%). Each factor is scored against your personal 30-day baseline, so a "good" HRV is good for you, not against a population average. Green (67 and above) means go, yellow (34–66) means train but keep it sensible, red (under 34) means prioritise rest.

Why don't I have a score yet?

The app needs a baseline. Wear your Apple Watch to sleep for at least three nights and the score appears. Sleep tracking must be on in the Watch app (Sleep → Track Sleep with Apple Watch) and the watch needs enough battery to last the night — charging it in the morning while you shower works well.

A factor shows "No data"

Check Health → Sharing → Apps → Restored and make sure every category is allowed. If a category is allowed but still empty, the watch did not record it last night. Two common catches: Apple only tracks sleep, HRV, respiratory rate and wrist temperature properly when a sleep schedule is set up (Health app → Sleep → Set Up Sleep) and the watch is worn to bed. And wrist temperature has an extra rule — after you first set up a sleep schedule, Apple spends about five nights calibrating before any readings appear at all, so give it most of a week before worrying.

Purchases and restoring

Restored is free for your first 7 days, then a single one-off purchase. If you reinstall or change phones, open the unlock screen and tap "Restore purchase" — it is tied to your Apple ID, not the device.
